How to play SynergyTower

You are the new hire at the bottom of a 40-floor corporate tower. Walk each floor, do the office chores as short mini-games, earn paperclips and KPI stars, impress HR and ride the elevator up, floor by floor, until the corner office on floor 40 is yours. This guide covers everything, from the lobby turnstiles to all 48 mini-games.

Play SynergyTower in your browser

The goal

The tower has 40 floors in eight departments of five floors each. Every floor you reach gives you a new job title, from Unpaid Intern in the lobby to Chief Executive Officer at the top. Your career is saved in your browser as you play, and the game works offline once loaded.

Your first day: the lobby

A new career starts outside the tower. Pick your company name and colour, your name and your look, then walk in. You arrive in the Grand Lobby with 50 paperclips, 5 Stock Options and an empty coffee pot.

Check in at the front desk

The receptionist waves you over. The front desk task is always the Jargon Decoder ("Decode the Memo") and it is free while you check in. Any result counts: finishing it fills your coffee pot, starts the refill and pays the usual rewards. It also unlocks the Signed In badge.

The turnstiles

The lobby turnstiles stay closed until you have checked in. The first time through, they take one cup of coffee from your new card. After that they are always free. Beyond them are the rest of the lobby floor and the elevator.

Tasks and KPI stars

Every floor has 8 workstations (rising to 14 on the top floors). A yellow ! marks a station that is ready. Walk up to it and press Space to open its task card, which shows the mini-game, its coffee cost, the rewards, your best result so far and how long the station rests after you play.

Each mini-game lasts up to about 90 seconds and ends with a score. The score turns into 0-3 KPI stars, and the stars decide the reward:

ScoreKPI starsPaperclips & XP
under 35%030% of the base reward
35-59%★100%
60-84%★★150%
85% and more★★★220%, plus a 5% chance of a bonus Stock Option

Rewards grow with the floor: a 1-star task pays 25 paperclips on floor 1, about 100 on floor 10 and over 2,000 on floor 30. Stars add up every time you play, so replaying a station is always worth it; your personal best per station is kept separately. You can pause any mini-game with Esc. Giving up scores 0.

Cooldowns

After you play, the station rests. Simple games come back slowly and complex ones quickly, so every game type is worth playing. The base cooldown is one minute on floor 1 and grows 8% per floor. A good home office day on floors 8, 18, 28 and 38 clears every station's cooldown on that floor.

Coffee

Coffee is your energy. Your pot holds 6 cups on the first floors and one more every five floors (14 on floor 40). It refills one cup every 2 minutes, even while the game is closed.

  • The coffee machine in the kitchen (most floors have one) pours up to 3 free cups once every 15 minutes, per floor. It fills your pot to the maximum, never past it; with a full pot it waits for you.
  • Promotions top your pot up to full.
  • The daily stand-up pays a cup, and winning the office cake race pays two (up to the maximum).
  • Holiday activities on vacation are the only coffee that goes over the maximum.
  • Stock Options: 3 SO refill the whole pot at once.
  • Perks: the Bigger Mug adds 2 cups per level and the Espresso Machine refills 10% faster per level.

Ran dry? You can switch on browser Reminders under Menu > Settings: the game then tells you when your coffee is full again (only while the tab is open in the background, at most 3 a day and never at night).

Getting promoted

To leave a floor you need four things. The elevator panel shows your progress on each one.

  1. KPI stars earned on this floor: 8 on floor 1, then 2 more per floor (26 on floor 10, 66 on floor 30).
  2. Seniority level of at least the next floor's number (level 10 to go to floor 10).
  3. The keycard fee in paperclips: 75 on floor 1, 1,233 on floor 10, about 76,000 on floor 30.
  4. The HR performance review. Once the first three are met, the HR office on the floor will see you.

The review costs 2 coffee: seven questions where you tell HR what they want to hear. Get 60% right to pass. If you fail, HR makes you wait (twice a station's base cooldown) before you can try again. Once you pass, take the elevator up: you pay the keycard fee and get a new floor, a new title, a full coffee pot and a Stock Option. Going down to floors you have already reached is always free.

On floors 3, 13, 23 and 33 there may be a shortcut: if you visit HR before you are promotion-ready, they might ask a favour that makes you ready on the spot. See the lost folder.

Paperclips, XP and Stock Options

  • Paperclips are the office currency. You earn them from tasks and spend them on keycard fees, perks, training and desk decorations.
  • XP fills your seniority level. Every level-up also pays a Stock Option. You need the right level for each promotion.
  • Stock Options (SO) are the premium currency. You start with 5 and earn more from level-ups, promotions, lucky 3-star results and daily stand-ups. Spend them to skip a cooldown (1 SO), refill your coffee (3 SO) or buy the two executive perks.

The bar at the top of the screen shows your paperclips, KPI stars against this floor's quota, your level, Stock Options, how many different mini-games you have played and your coffee with the time to the next cup. Click any of them for details.

Floor quests

Two stations send you out onto the floor before the desk part starts. A timer runs at the top of the screen; G gives up.

  • 360 Feedback: find the named colleague, chat with them, then return to the station and fill in their review from what they told you.
  • Lost Password: your password is your pet's name. Search the floor's temporary whiteboards for your pet's photo, then log back in.

The daily stand-up

Once a day (after your check-in), a colleague on your floor has a blue speech bubble: they need a quick favour before stand-up. The favour is one of the quick mini-games and it is completely free: no coffee, no cooldown and it does not count towards your KPI stars. In return you get double paperclips, XP, a cup of coffee and a 20% chance of a Stock Option. Do it seven days in a row and the seventh day pays a Stock Option for sure.

Office events

Office life is unpredictable. After you have spent some time on a floor, a random event can break out (roughly every 6-10 minutes). Winning pays a bonus; losing costs nothing.

Business trips

Floors 10 and 30 hide a secret opportunity: a business trip to a client. How you get it is up to you to find out. Once you do, the pool car is yours for a drive of about three minutes through town, the countryside, the motorway and a business park. Each checkpoint adds time to the clock. Overtake the traffic, slow down for the sharp bends and keep off the verge. Hitting a car or a lamp post at speed is a crash: the car spins out, loses its speed and takes a dent (the DMG gauge under the stage name, top left). It can take four; the fifth crash wrecks it. A wreck or running out of time ends the trip for good. Every trip is a one-time offer.

Make it, and you have 15 minutes in the client's office, a much bigger floor than any in the tower. Tasks there cost the usual coffee but pay double paperclips (no KPI stars, it is not your floor), and the client's coffee brewers are ready again every 2 minutes. When the clock runs out, the pool car takes you back to the office. Had enough? Walk out through the main entrance (the green EXIT in the lobby) at any time and you are straight back on your floor.

Vacation

Floor 20 hides another secret: a holiday at the Paradise Palms Resort, a tropical five-star hotel. How you get there is up to you to find out. Once you do, you have 15 minutes of ultra all inclusive: sleep in your suite, raid the dinner buffet, bounce a beach ball in the pool, sip a cocktail at the tiki bar, get a massage in the spa cabana, then head through the garden to the beach to sunbathe, build a sandcastle or swim out to the buoy from the jetty. No office work allowed.

The activities are free and easy mini-games (see Holiday activities). Enjoy one (1 star or more) and you take an extra cup of coffee back to the office, once per activity and stay. That coffee goes on top of a full pot and stays there until you drink it. Want to get back to work early? Check out through the front door (the green EXIT in the lobby).

The night shift

On floors 5, 15, 25 and 35 your manager is looking for a volunteer to hold the fort tonight. Say yes and everybody (HR included) heads for the elevator, the lights go out and the office is yours for 10 minutes: a few ceiling lamps still glow (some of them flicker), and the music turns a lot more dramatic.

At night the stations come back twice as fast (half the cooldown, including the ones already waiting when the shift starts) and the floor's coffee machine brews a fresh round every 2 minutes. Tasks pay the usual paperclips, XP and KPI stars. Anything that needs colleagues is closed until morning: HR reviews, floor quests, the stand-up, office events and the coffee break chat. One shift a day, for all four floors together. The elevator only offers to send you home early; either way you are back in the daytime office with everyone in, and a card sums up your night.

Home office

On floors 8, 18, 28 and 38 your manager thinks you look exhausted: "Finish it from home today." Say yes and you are home in your own little flat for 10 minutes: a living room with your computer, a kitchen and a bathroom. One home office day a day, for all four floors together, and it costs nothing.

The catch: your boss watches your status. The bar chart over your computer is your presence meter; it runs down while you walk about and while you do your chores, and runs out after about two and a half minutes. Pop over to the computer now and then to wiggle the mouse. If it runs dry, your status goes Away. Once or twice a day the boss also calls: "Camera on in 20 s!" Get to the computer in time; a missed call counts as going Away too. Three of those and the boss calls you back to the office for the rest of the day.

Meanwhile, the chores (see Home chores): vacuuming, the dishes, the laundry, the ironing and a bath. Each costs 1 coffee and pays XP and a few paperclips the first time you get 1 star or more that day, but never KPI stars. Out of coffee? Brew your own: the coffee maker in the kitchen is free and pours 3 cups (never past your mug's maximum). The better you brew, the sooner it is ready again (2 to 4 minutes). Head back whenever you like through the front door. Come back on good terms (fewer than three lapses) and every station on that floor is ready for you, cooldowns and all cleared. (Like the business trip, the holiday and the night shift, the clock stands still while the game is paused.)

The lost folder

On floors 3, 13, 23 and 33, HR has a favour to ask if you drop by before you are promotion-ready: a personnel folder went missing in the archive. Say yes and the elevator gets an extra button, A, down to the basement: one huge, dark room of filing cabinets, lit by a few tired ceiling lamps and your own little light. The folder is in one drawer somewhere at the far end, and you have 5 minutes from the moment the doors open (the clock turns red for the last 15 seconds). Going through a cabinet takes about a second; the ones you have checked are ticked off.

Get back into the elevator with the folder in time and take it to HR on your floor: they grant exactly what your promotion still lacks (KPI stars, the level, the keycard fee), and then it is review time as usual. Run out of time, or give up at the elevator, and you are back upstairs with nothing else lost, but the chance is gone: HR asks only once per floor, so saying no is final too.

Up on the roof

The tower has a roof, and some of your colleagues know the way up. How you get there is up to you to find out (keep your ears open). Up there: the best view in the company, a pay telescope with a few sights to spot, the window cleaners' cradle, and a bench where the smokers gossip. They know things: when something is about to happen downstairs, which station frees up next, and maybe where something lost has been hidden. The sky follows your local time, from blue to sunset to the city lit up at night.

Nothing up there pays. And the roof is closed to staff: now and then someone from facilities comes out of the roof door for a look round. Keep out of his sight. If he catches you, he walks you back down, and the way up stays shut for the rest of the day (no HR strike: he was up there for a cigarette himself).

Office conduct

On every floor one colleague stops for a coffee break chat when you talk to them: small talk, or ask them out for a drink. You get a polite (and final) no, and HR makes a note: every ask is a strike. Ask again and the answers get colder, colleagues start talking, and the card keeps count in its HR notes. Strikes add up across all colleagues and floors.

The 5th strike puts you on compliance hold: your tasks, the elevator and the workstations are taped off until you take the free Compliance & Ethics training at any HR office (see the training). Pass it (70% of the answers right) and your record is cleared. Fail it and you are demoted one floor, and the HR performance review there is due again. On the ground floor there is no floor below: failing there gets you fired, and that career is over. (So does getting reported before you have even checked in at reception: no badge, no way to HR.) Otherwise your strikes start from zero, so it can all happen again, on any floor. Except with the colleague who reported you: they will never be asked again. Lesson learned? We will see.

The Christmas party

Every year from 6 to 23 December the Fun Committee throws the office Christmas party. The first time you step onto a floor in those weeks, their invitation pops up: Go now, or Later, and the elevator keeps a red P · Christmas party button until you go. There is one party per career per year, so once it has started, that year's is used up. (You need your badge from the front desk, no compliance hold, and not to be away on a trip, a holiday, a night shift, a home office day or down in the archive.)

The party lasts 10 minutes in a hall of its own: a dance floor under a disco ball and sweeping coloured lights, a DJ booth, a stage, a buffet, a bar, a big tree and a photo booth, with confetti and Santa hats everywhere. The guests are colleagues you know from the floors you have worked on, and some of them are already dancing. The CEO opens it with a speech, and the year-end bonus is 1 Stock Option for coming. Five activities are free (no coffee), and each one pays double what a 3-star task pays on your highest floor, the first time you get 1 star or more at it (never XP or KPI stars):

  • Dance-Off at the DJ booth: hit the arrows as they cross the line, on the beat of the party tune.
  • Secret Santa at the table by the tree: wrap your gift (cut the paper, tape the seam, tie the bow). Do it with at least 1 star and your own Secret Santa gift is waiting: that year's little Christmas tree for your desk, which no shop sells.
  • Carol Karaoke on the stage: keep your voice on the lane of the note being sung.
  • Christmas Buffet: All You Can Eat, with turkey, mince pies and gingerbread.
  • Photo Booth: four shots; strike the pose on each card (a fresh press for every card) and hold it when the flash goes off.

The bar is free too: mulled wine, eggnog and Synergy Punch. Drinks pay nothing; they only make the room sway (and the mini-games with it), and after the third you start seeing double and the bartender pours water only. Water sobers you up at once, and it wears off by itself, one drink every two minutes. Leave by the elevator doors whenever you like; either way you are back on your floor with a summary of the night. (Like the other timed stays, the clock stands still while the game is paused.)

Your desk

Every floor you reach has a desk with your nameplate, marked MY DESK until you decorate it. Your decorations move with you from floor to floor: one on the left, one on the right and one on top of the monitor. Each floor's shop sells its own selection of seven items (always some stationery) plus that floor's "Best Employee" trophy, which you can only buy after meeting the floor's KPI star quota. Swap what is on show any time in the Collection tab.

Floor 40: the CEO

Reach the top floor and you are the Chief Executive Officer. You can now decide your colleagues' fate: talk to anyone on floor 40 and choose Fire, Keep or Later. Fire ten people for the Restructuring badge, or the whole board for Clean Sweep.

Extras

  • The workstation terminal. One desk on every floor shows a >_ prompt. It opens a terminal; type help and see what a curious employee can do.
  • Hallway posters. Every floor has a "YOUR LOGO HERE" spot and a (fake) ad. If you would like a real poster in the game, get in touch.
  • Poke around. Most things in the office react when you walk up and press Space or click them, even without the gold brackets that mark gameplay. Chairs, sofas, toilets and server racks get a comment, a different one at random each time. The fridge opens: read the labels on everybody's lunch and keep an eye on the container at the back. Plants have care tags and watering logs; some have a worm, some hide lost things in the soil. The vending machine takes imaginary coins: pick a snack and watch the spiral turn. Sometimes it drops, sometimes it gets stuck and needs a polite nudge, and sometimes you get something else entirely. In the HR office, the filing cabinets hold a personnel file for every colleague on this and the neighbouring floors, and one about you. None of it costs coffee or earns anything; it is just there to be found. A station with a task that is also a fridge or a plant has a Peek button on its task card.
  • Several careers. A browser keeps up to 100 careers side by side. Continue career on the title screen shows them all as employee ID badges, last played first: pick one with the arrow keys or a click, start a New career, or Delete one you are done with (it asks twice). To switch careers, use "Save & quit to title" in the menu. With 100 careers saved, delete one before you start another.
  • The menu (Esc) has Settings (music, sound effects and reminders), the quick guide, feedback and "Save & quit to title".

All the mini-games

There are 48 mini-games. 33 of them are shared out across the tower's stations, so every floor has a different mix. They come in three types; the type sets the coffee cost and the cooldown. Of the other 15, 8 are holiday activities, waiting for you to find them, 6 are home chores for a home office day, and one is a smoke break on the roof. Every game has a time limit of about 90 seconds or less, and works with the keyboard, the mouse and touch. The screenshots show the Customer Support versions; other departments use their own words and props.

Quick tasks: 1 coffee (11 games)

Reflex games and one-choice-per-round games. Cheap to play, but the station then rests the longest (4x the base cooldown).

Badge Tailgate 1 coffee

Guard the badge gate. Stop anyone without a badge beep or a VISITOR sticker before they get through; stopping legitimate staff costs you.

Controls: Stop people without a badge (green BEEP) or VISITOR sticker before they pass the gate. SPACE stops the marked person (Left/Right to change), or click a person.

Boss Alert 1 coffee

You are playing solitaire at work. Alt-tab right before the boss with the red tie walks past your desk; false alarms cost you.

Controls: Press SPACE (or click) to alt-tab right before the BOSS (red tie) passes your desk. False alarms cost you.

Corporate Quiz 1 coffee

Corporate trivia with a ticking clock: pick the best answer to each question.

Controls: Pick the best answer before time runs out. Keys 1-4 or arrows + Space, or click.

Elevator Pitch 1 coffee

Thirty seconds in the elevator with the CEO. Stop each slot reel on the word the CEO will love; the rumour at the top tells you what they like.

Controls: Stop each reel (Space/Enter, click STOP or the reel) on the word the CEO will love. Follow the rumour icon!

Expense Report 1 coffee

Receipts versus budget. Add up the receipts and pick how much budget is left from four amounts.

Controls: Add up the receipts and pick the right amount. Keys 1-4 or arrows + Space, or click.

Inbox Triage 1 coffee

Mail, tickets and requests drop into your inbox one after another. Sort each one into the right of three bins before it slips past: left is for what needs you, the middle for what is kept for later, right for junk. The bins follow the department (Reply / Archive / Spam for mail, Solve / Escalate / Close in Customer Support) and each one shows its rule, e.g. "needs an answer" under Reply. A mail's sender is a good clue.

Controls: Sort each item into the right bin. Keys: ← ↓ → (or 1 2 3), or click a bin.

Jargon Decoder 1 coffee

The boss says a buzzword. Pick what it REALLY means.

Controls: The boss says a buzzword. Pick what it REALLY means. Keys 1-4 or arrows + Space, or click.

Meeting Buzzword Bingo 1 coffee

Sit through a meeting with a buzzword bingo card. Mark a word the moment someone says it and complete as many lines as you can.

Controls: When someone says a word on your card, press Space or click it. Complete the lines!

Perfect Timing 1 coffee

Fix the jammed printer: mash to fill the power bar, then strike when the needle is in the green zone (yellow counts half).

Controls: First MASH Space (or click) to fill the bar, then press it when the needle is in the green zone. Yellow = half.

Schedule the Meeting 1 coffee

Busy colleagues, one meeting. Find the time slot where every attendee is free and book it, round after round.

Controls: Find the slot where everyone is free. ← → to choose, Space to book, or click the column.

Whack-a-Bug 1 coffee

Bugs (or tickets) pop up on a 3x3 grid. Hit them before they escape, but spare the decoys like thank-you notes.

Controls: Hit targets as they pop up: numpad keys 7-9/4-6/1-3, arrows + SPACE, or click. Spare the decoys!

Medium tasks: 2 coffee (13 games)

Memory, typing and juggling games. Two cups, 1.5x the base cooldown.

Coffee Crossing 2 coffee

Carry your coffee from the kitchen to your desk across the busy office floor without bumping into anyone. Hold an arrow key to keep hopping.

Controls: Carry your coffee from the kitchen to your desk. Arrows hop (hold to keep going) or click beside you. Dodge traffic!

Coffee Run 2 coffee

Memorise every colleague's coffee order (size, drink, milk), then rebuild each order from memory.

Controls: Memorise every order, then rebuild each one. Keys 1-4, arrows + Space, or click.

Desk Plant Survival 2 coffee

Keep the desk plant alive for a quarter: water and light in the green bands, blinds open or closed, pests sprayed.

Controls: Keep water and light in the green bands and squash pests. 1/W water, 2/B blinds, 3/SPACE spray, or click.

Expense Fraud Detective 2 coffee

Expense claims under the microscope. Approve the legitimate receipts and reject the fraud: check dates, amounts, reasons and duplicates.

Controls: Check each receipt: APPROVE (A / Right / click) the legit ones, REJECT (R / Left / click) the fraud. Watch for duplicates!

Meeting Survival 2 coffee

Stay awake in a long meeting: sip coffee to keep your eyelids up (not too often, or the jitters get you) and nod only when a buzzword is said.

Controls: Stay awake: SPACE or click the mug to sip (not too often!). Nod with N / Up / NOD button ONLY at buzzwords.

Name Badge Memory 2 coffee

Name badges lie face down. Flip two at a time and match the pairs in as few turns as possible.

Controls: Flip two badges at a time and match the pairs. Arrows + Space, or click.

PowerPoint Karaoke 2 coffee

Present slides you have never seen. For each chart, pick the narration that actually matches it.

Controls: Present slides you have never seen: pick the narration that matches the chart. Keys 1-4, arrows + Space, or click.

Quarterly Ticker 2 coffee

A live share price. Buy when it is low, sell when it is high and reach the profit target before the closing bell.

Tips

  • Mix the game types: complex games cost more coffee but come back quickly, quick games are cheap but rest for a while.
  • Stars add up, so a 2-star replay often beats waiting for a perfect run.
  • Visit the coffee machine on the floors you pass: each one has its own 15-minute timer.
  • Do the daily stand-up first: it is free and pays double.
  • Short on level but rich in paperclips? The Training Center closes the gap.
  • The Bigger Mug and Espresso Machine are the best early perks: more coffee means more tasks per session.
